# Scene: Sick in the Room

Captured from the "epiphany / dream" voice note. There's a beat sheet first, then a rough prose draft.

## Why this scene works

It shows how the sick fights **once it's exposed** ("you're acting weird, game over, now it has to kill"), using two rules:

1. **Bodies before fighters.** Sick ignores whoever is attacking it and goes for the corpse, because a corpse is a new sick.
2. **It mirrors.** The new sick fights *exactly* like the human who just hit it, because it's running on that human's history and reflexes. That includes copying what the humans do to wake each other up.

Every human who runs in to help just adds bodies. The room fills up and the scene ends there.

## Cast (placeholder names)

- **The Worker** is alone in the room, organizing. It's already sick and fully disguised. Call it **Sick 1**.
- **The Rusher** is the first to burst in. They know. They die and become **Sick 2**.
- **The Second** comes in, tries to beat the Rusher awake and gets mirrored.
- **The Third**, then **two more**: the group at the doorway.

## Beat sheet

1. A small room with a 3 m ceiling, a normal house. The Worker is organizing, calm, at work.
2. The Rusher charges in. Punches drive the Worker into the wall, then kicks, kicks, a pummeling.
3. The Worker **drops** under a kick, nonchalant, already charging a punch. It doesn't dodge. It goes to the floor and swings an **uppercut** that launches the Rusher.
4. Someone says **"Sick."** (Ideally the Rusher, as recognition. Or the Worker, naming itself.)
5. The Worker slides across the floor under the falling Rusher, **stands bolt upright** and headbutts them into the ceiling. The Rusher bounces off and falls.
6. On the way down, the Worker winds up to drive the Rusher's head into the floor. **The Rusher dies.**
7. The Second runs in and **kicks** Sick 1 into the wall.
8. Sick 1 doesn't fight the Second. It **kicks off the wall, straight to the Rusher's corpse** and starts beating it.
9. The Second kicks Sick 1 again, and this time it's **knocked out.** The Second drops to the Rusher and starts hitting them to wake them up.
10. The corpse **wakes up as Sick 2.** It grabs the Second, throws them down and pummels them **exactly the way the Second was hitting it**, same rhythm, same hands.
11. The Third runs in. Sick 2 **throws the Second into the Third** and in the same motion **leaps onto Sick 1's body and beats it awake**, the way the Second tried to wake the Rusher. (Mirror again.)
12. Two more humans run in. Sick 2 keeps pummeling.
13. Sick 2 **picks up the Worker's body** and **throws it** into the group at the door. They all go down tangled together, and they're all sick.
14. **Cut.**

## Rough draft

The room is small. Three metres to the ceiling, plaster gone soft at the corners. The Worker sits on the floor surrounded by little stacks: papers, wire, jars with handwritten labels. It moves one stack two centimetres to the left and considers it.

The door doesn't open. It comes off.

The Rusher is already moving when the hinges hit the floor. The first punch takes the Worker in the jaw and the second puts it into the wall, and the wall answers with a crack. The Rusher doesn't stop to look. Knee. Elbow. A kick to the ribs, another, another, the rhythm of someone who has practised this in their head every night for weeks.

The Worker lets it happen.

Then, on the fourth kick, it simply isn't there. It drops the way you'd sit down to tie a shoe, unhurried, and its fist is already coming up off the floor. The uppercut catches the Rusher under the chin and lifts them off their feet.

"Sick," the Rusher says, or tries to. It comes out with blood.

The Worker slides across the floor beneath them and stands up all at once, a spring released, and its forehead meets the Rusher's chest on the way up. The Rusher hits the ceiling. Plaster rains down. They hang there for half a breath, then fall.

The Worker is already turning, fist cocked low, waiting for the head to come down to meet the floor.

It does.

Footsteps. The Second comes through the doorway at a sprint and puts a boot into the Worker's side, and the Worker goes into the wall. It spins as it lands and plants its feet on the plaster.

It doesn't look at the Second. It looks at the body.

It kicks off the wall like a swimmer off the end of a pool and lands on the Rusher's corpse, fists already going. The Second screams something and kicks it again, square in the temple. The Worker folds over and stays down.

The Second falls to their knees by the Rusher. "Come on. Come on." Open palm across the face. Again. A fist to the chest, hard, the way you'd restart a heart. "Wake up. *Wake up.*"

The Rusher's eyes open.

Their hand closes on the Second's collar and throws them flat on their back, and then the Rusher is on top, hitting them. Open palm across the face. Again. A fist to the chest, hard.

"Wake up," it says, in the Rusher's voice. "*Wake up.*"

The Third is in the doorway. The thing that was the Rusher looks up, takes the Second by the shirt and throws them across the room into the Third. Both go down. It's already moving the other way. It drops onto the Worker's slack body and starts beating it: palm, palm, fist to the chest.

Two more come through the door and stop.

It's still hitting. Palm. Palm. Fist.

The Worker's body stirs. The Rusher stands, lifts it under the arms like a sack of feed, turns and throws it at the doorway.

Four people go down under the weight of it, all of them tangled, in the dark of the hall.

Cut.

## Open questions

- **Who says "Sick"?** If the Rusher says it, they knew all along and came in to kill it. If the Worker says it, it's a name or a taunt.
- **Where does this go?** Options: a cold open before Mary Sue, the moment Mary Sue walks into, or something she hears about secondhand. It also shows *why* she trusts nobody.
- **Infection rule:** is it death plus contact? Does the beating spread it, or the blood? The throw that "makes them all sick at the same time" needs a rule.
- **Is the Worker a clone kid grown up?** That would tie it to the clone nursery in the center of human territory.
