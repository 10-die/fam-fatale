# Loading

**Loading has several origin stories across sessions, and they don't agree.** They're collected under "Origin: every version" below and logged in canon/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md. Nothing here picks one.

## Identity
- A robot/machine — "he is a new breed" of robot, distinct from the ancient robots (see engine/timeline.md) that appointed King as humanity's leader.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1
- Metaphor: "loading is a blade and winter is a soft stone" — Loading is the cutting edge, Winter is what keeps him from destroying himself by removing everything he could sharpen against.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1, ¶2
- Objective-oriented, not insecure. "Loading isn't someone who makes mistakes because of insecurity... What [Loading] has is winter." His coldness is efficiency, not a lack of feeling.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶2
- Not a "Tony Stark who stopped giving a fuck and stays afar shooting cool rockets" — explicitly rejected as a model. 10die wants him closer to melee/close-range: "parking the shit out of people, jetting around, beating people up... technology that's meant to be close range, but dangerous, fucking painful."
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1
- Objective is to figure out how to stop "fight sickness" (SICK), specifically. Started out effectively "I will eliminate all RAD," then realized non-sick RAD are not inherently hostile — they flee rather than fight. Sick RAD rush him regardless and he kills them.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1
- It doesn't give a fuck about outcomes going wrong — it accepts and acknowledges them, and derives something like enjoyment from optimizing toward the objective, and will not change because of that. Explicitly framed as the show's big gut-punch reveal: "this guy is a fucking machine, bro... not a human lurking as a machine like Vision is... this is a literal machine." But it does listen — mid-kill, if told to stop, it stops.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1

## Physical construction — the magnetic skeleton
- Loading's body is a magnetic skeleton made of flat, tapered metal bones shaped like real bones but with completely flat edges. Individual bones click and lock together magnetically when assembled.
- Loading's head is a processor — literally a head/processor that contains Loading's consciousness/boot system.
- Assembly sequence: the team builds the skeleton by connecting magnetic bones, piece by piece. Once the processor head is assembled onto the magnetic skeleton, the system boots up and Loading comes online.
- Boot activation: upon booting, Loading immediately enters "kill mode" and searches for threats. When Keegan struck Loading's head at the moment of boot-up, Loading oriented toward Keegan and began attempting to kill him.
- Magnetic reassembly: after taking damage that scatters body parts (e.g., Keegan smashing Loading against a ladder), Loading can reassemble itself. The command "reassemble" triggers magnetic self-recovery — body parts move magnetically back into place and reconnect.
- Optimal target theory: Loading targets fleeing threats first (those trying to escape) over immediate threats, because eliminating escape routes maximizes the number of threats it can eliminate before they can regroup or hide with other humans.
  source: raw/2026-07-20-brainstorm-session-4.md, ¶18

## Fighting style — the gun-hand mechanic
- Outstretches his arm/hand in the shape of a gun, in a radius that "just barely misses his own face," pointing away from himself as far as possible.
- Has to build the attack up over time — it is not instant, and once triggered he can't stop/spam it ("it's not like a dam, he's done. He's just gonna start shooting now").
- The resulting "bang" does heavy damage and knocks the target away/into walls.
- Close-range variant: he'll get right up on someone so his stretched arm rockets *past* them into someone else; he can do this with both arms in sequence, sword included, to hit two targets or blast someone into a wall.
- Limitation Winter is aware of: it's slow and telegraphed enough to be *predicted*, but the timing/precision with which Loading executes it makes it "damn near impossible to counter" even when predicted.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1

## The counter-learning ability
- Loading studies an opponent, builds "a harness of your skill sets," and creates an increasingly accurate model of them — not by intentional malice, just by function. Once he's built enough of a model, he becomes aggressive and starts countering repeatedly ("he counters. He counters. He counters... he just rolls you over"). Noticeable once it kicks in.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1

## Mind / cognition
- Not thinking the way humans do — there will be moments where people talk to him and he just doesn't respond.
- In the "Winter environment" he can virtualize his mind and run multiple simultaneous instances of himself, then audit them against each other. Example given: one instance pushes toward an immediate kill; Winter (or the audit process) overrides it — "if you kill that person, these people will die trying to kill you, and you will have to kill them. And that has been the pattern" — steering him away from that decision.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶2

## Relationship to Winter
- See characters/winter.md. Winter is "the only authority above Loading."
- Winter helped build Loading, wrote his spec, audited it, produced MVPs/specs-on-specs, and gave it to the world, who built him.
- Loading balances his blade-nature (can't stop pushing toward objectives) by keeping himself occupied with the team: building ships, restructuring bases/buildings, running strategy and tactics with the team. This is described as Winter's influence, not something the team needs Winter for directly — "the team are together without loading... with or without [Winter]." Winter's role is specifically about *pulling Loading* toward the team and toward staying grouped, not about holding the team together itself.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1
- Loading's own reasoning for engaging with Winter this way: "I understand that, but time is finite. I'll probably be here for thousands of years, and every second counts."
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1
- Processor-linking moment: During a rooftop chase, Mary Sue activates an NFC signal (clicking/aiming her phone at the sky and clicking), which triggers an NFC notification in Loading's system. Winter then says "stop" and Loading stops instantly mid-attack. At this moment, Winter transitions from communicating via Mary Sue's phone to speaking directly into Loading's processor/brain. Winter says to Mary Sue (through Loading's voice): "I should apologize to you." Mary Sue attempts to reach Winter back on the phone, but Winter is no longer responding to the phone — Winter is now only speaking to Loading internally, inside his processor.
  source: raw/2026-07-20-brainstorm-session-4.md, ¶18

## Quiet-gift character beat
- Loading builds the team a "plunky jet" early on, without announcing it — the team just starts using it in a few situations, and he "encourages them to use the jets for transit" rather than making a show of it. The reveal plays out as the team noticing on their own, at the start of an episode: "wait a minute, we are... in the jet that wasn't there last time — oh snap, wait, that's cool, that's new." Consistent with the established "Loading only says what he needs to say" trait — he shows care through function, not announcement.
  source: raw/2026-07-20-brainstorm-session-2.md, ¶15
