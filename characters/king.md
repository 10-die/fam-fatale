# King

See OPEN_QUESTIONS.md for the unresolved question of King's scope (national office vs. supra-national figurehead).

## Identity
- Leader of humanity, appointed by the ancient robots (the "custodians of humanity" — see engine/timeline.md) as leader of "the group," and humanity in turn chooses that group as their leaders "because it works" / because they organize things.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1
- This dump refers to him as "King of humans," and separately describes "the king of humans... just roaming on a planet somewhere, every country has its own stable government" — distinct from a specific national head of state.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶9, ¶1
- Distinct from "the president of america Sten menli," who "flies around America in the Great bald eagle" — named separately and given a distinct vehicle/vibe, suggesting King and the US president are two different offices in this dump (contributes to, but doesn't fully resolve, the open King-scope question).
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1
- Cannot personally order an abduction from the White House the way Winter can — Winter helped King build the White House specifically so it retains the capacity to contain *anything*, including "a rogue Loading." King is bound by that design; Winter isn't.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1
- Doesn't need to be physically present for major arc-1 decisions — e.g. the room where Winter's terminal is rebooted proceeds without him.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1

## Origin — was the President of the United States
- Major new lore, only surfaced in a single dense parenthetical aside: King "was the president of the United States," who "destroyed the constitution" and then audited it, using the audits, and livestreamed everything to every citizen "on the land of America."
- A livestreamed, crowd-resolved immigration policy is given as the founding example of how King governs: after heated live debate, it's resolved that "if you are standing on the soil of the United States of America, you are a citizen thereof" with the full rights and responsibilities of citizenship, because citizens "all share... ownership of their government." 10die's own aside here — "King, the only one percenter" — is unclear in meaning (possibly that King alone retains some reserved authority/stake while citizenship/ownership is otherwise shared 99/1; not explained further) and shouldn't be over-interpreted.
- Framed as a choice rather than a mandate: become a documented individual, use the systems (groceries, schools, roads), pay taxes, and in exchange get a vote in "the American Life Stream" and its petitions/polls — persuade enough neighbors and you get what 10die calls "a park" (likely garbled — possibly "pardon" or "path [to citizenship]"; preserved as spoken, not resolved).
- This American precedent becomes "a prelude" — South Korea's leaders start discussing the same governing approach, which is what gets King "elected to presidency" there too, and then — in 10die's word — King is "incarnated" (i.e., instantiated/crowned) as King more broadly. Read together with the pre-existing "appointed by the ancient custodian robots" origin (¶1 of the first dump), this may describe two different stages — first elected head of state via this livestream-democracy model in multiple countries, then formally installed as the supra-national King — rather than a contradiction; not confirmed by 10die as such, so treat as an open thread rather than a settled sequence. See OPEN_QUESTIONS.md.
  source: raw/2026-07-20-brainstorm-session-2.md, ¶16

I would not be my king — I would be your king. If I want to do something and the stream is telling me no, stop — I can[not] continue, take my power. If you think I'm being abusive, take my power. ## Governing philosophy — the "head must be a servant" model
- Explicit rejection of leading from a hill and only issuing commands downward. 10die's own metaphor: a body where "the head" tries to prove to the rest of the body that it's more powerful, versus the model King is meant to embody — "the head must be a servant, the head must not be able to do anything but delegate and report and command."
- Governing inputs described as going beyond raw economic output — citizen struggles get tied to data alongside the numbers: how much revenue something like cigarette sales brings in *and* how many humans are being lost to it, efficiency, happiness, whether people have things to do, a sense of community/village, and "the deep sense of national pri[de]" — explicitly contrasted with governing that's about *appearing* in control rather than actually understanding citizens.
  source: raw/2026-07-20-brainstorm-session-2.md, ¶16
- The physical-travel requirement is framed as necessary specifically so King doesn't become one of the leaders he's critiquing: "the king does not sit at the top of the building wondering what to do... king is in the field... moving around the planet." Direct quote of King's own stated itinerary: "I'm going from Kenya to Tanzania to Zimbabwe to [South] Korea."
- There's a harder edge to this same passage that reads as a warning about a *failure mode*, not a description of King succeeding: a king who has "every possible need taken care of" for him, who never actually shops, drives the same roads, or plays at the same park as citizens, ends up understood only "in the capacity of danger... or instilled fear" rather than being loved. 10die's stated conclusion is that King "needs to have insane resilience and capacity to interact socially with many different people" — i.e., the travel has to be real contact, not a hollow tour. Preserved close to as-spoken since the passage is dense and could be read more than one way; not simplified into a single flat claim.
  source: raw/2026-07-20-brainstorm-session-2.md, ¶16

## Surveillance and command power
- King spies on humans and is "always live streaming" — described as both the defining power and the "caveat" of being King. 10die is emphatic here: "there is no leader in fiction or reality who ha[s] as much control over their subjects as King does — when King says to do something, it is done." Confrontation over an unpopular decision happens *after* the fact, not before — King has to be able to command immediately, and that trust comes from humanity's love for him, not from formal restraint.
- Illustrative vignette (compressed — the raw passage is heavily garbled by voice-transcription): King is shown casually spying on ordinary people for amusement (watching, laughing) when he spots someone on a bicycle heading home about to be ambushed at their gate/garage — viewers in his livestream chat are confused why the doors are behaving strangely — and King, watching, also sees who else is connected to that person (their circle) and realizes they're "currently in a fight for their life." King then acts: he commands humanity to move on the SICK threat.
- The human response to that command has no formal chain of command — it's just "the whole of humans in the area... shifting toward" the threat, mass and immediate rather than organized. Even if the human who engages first dies, the SICK RAD "will die there," because if a SICK target isn't neutralized ("lobotomized," in 10die's word — destroying whatever is hosting SICK, see mechanics/sick.md), the stakes escalate fast: "2 sick can equal a war," and at that scale it stops being a matter of designated fighters — "everyone can fight, everyone must fight." SICK has to be dealt with swiftly inside humanity's borders, or it costs a whole state to the RAD.
  source: raw/2026-07-20-brainstorm-session-2.md, ¶14
