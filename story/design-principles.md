# Design Principles

Rules of thumb that keep coming up across the brainstorms. If a scene breaks one of these, it should be on purpose.

## The tool is the weapon

> *"That's the type of thinking I want in this story, in this show, in whatever it ends up being."*

Don't bolt weapons onto things. **Find the version where the thing itself hits.**
- **Jars** rams with the plane's nose. The plane bounces off a building and the building reacts as if the plane went through it. ([jars.md](../characters/jars.md))
- **Keegan** in a jet: *"I know I could just be a show of force, but I prefer to be a weapon. My own weapon."* ([scene seed](scene-seeds.md#keegan-flies-a-fighter-jet))
- **Loading's finger gun** fires a punch, and he uses it to move himself and others as well as to attack. ([loading.md](../characters/loading.md))
- **A rad's greeting is a fist.**

## Momentum is character

How someone moves *is* who they are.
- **Keegan** is always moving toward a fight, and is very hard to dodge.
- **Mary Sue** dodges everything.
- **Jars** never stops. He only redirects.
- **Loading** has to *time* his movement. Calling the wings too early or too late is a real cost.

## Show it, never announce it

**We're never told who the greatest is. That's cheating.** Their impact should be undeniable on screen.

## No protagonist, no antagonist

The camera relays between groups, and it stays with the loser. ([narrative-structure.md](narrative-structure.md))

## Horror without gloom

**The sick territories are fun.** McConnell is a sitcom with murder in every episode, not a dreary horror sludge-fest. Rad are having a blast everywhere.

## Scale you can read

Wide, Godzilla-fight-scale framing, **but the fighters are human-sized** and you can still pick out every one of them. Cut to cockpit or close-up to show the effort it takes. ([Jars arrives](scene-seeds.md#jars-arrives-on-earth))
