## The wedding-lake sequence
- A wedding at a lake, sunset, on a hill with a dock leading down to the water. The couple are RAD who are "pushing SICK" — building hype around dying.
- They say vows loudly enough for the whole crowd to hear, then drop to a quieter, private register mid-vow — the crowd debates among themselves whether the couple knows they can still be heard, or whether that's the point.
- Mid-vow, someone shoots the bride hundreds of meters into the lake (through the trees without hitting them).
- The groom (10die doesn't settle on how to refer to them — "the motherfucker," full of rage, willing to die) sees a winged, jet-propelled human shooter and immediately jets after them, diving toward the water.
- The groom gasps for air after hitting the water, looks up, and sees Loading closing in — too late. Loading knocks them out mid-air, does a 360, dives into the water, retrieves the body, and kills them.
- Loading then super-jets in the lake, creating a wave that hits the entire wedding reception up on the hill.
- Loading flies off to find someone else. The Boeing jet (see forces/winter-team.md) is elsewhere, "trying to figure out who fucked with Winter" — implying Winter's disappearance/interference is happening concurrently with this sequence.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1

## The Chad-city sequence
- Loading arrives at a well-developed city (10die uses "Chad" as a placeholder example, not necessarily a confirmed place name) to kill someone else. People there try to attack him. Right as he's about to land the kill, Loading is interrupted.
- The interruption: someone is abducted from the White House "per Winter directive" — a move King himself can't make, because Winter helped build the White House specifically to retain the ability to contain anything, including a rogue Loading (see characters/king.md, characters/winter.md).
- 10die frames this as one of the show's biggest gut-punch reveals about Loading: it doesn't regret or change course because of what happened — it accepts and acknowledges the outcome, and derives something like enjoyment from optimizing toward the objective regardless. It's explicitly not "a human lurking as a machine like Vision is" — it's a literal machine that nonetheless will stop mid-kill if told to.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1

## "Winter's missing" episode-ending beat
- Loading fires away; Winter goes missing/silent. The jump jet and Boeing jet (autonomous, "anxiety built into them") get anxious and chase after Loading on their own, without instruction, unable to reach Winter for guidance.
- Episode ends on a view from space: something crossing the sky, visibly disturbing cloud patterns, heading toward Canada. The reveal that whoever/whatever this is heads toward Canada triggers panic because of who's known to be there — cold open into the next episode.
- This "person" is later described as someone who has been "rebating" (challenging/opposing) both Loading and King, while a lot of humans are trying to integrate peacefully with RAD and manage SICK. Not further identified in this dump.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1
