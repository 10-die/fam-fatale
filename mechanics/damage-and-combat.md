# Damage & combat — core system

## The core rule
- Nobody dies clean, in one hit. A sword slash to the neck won't kill you — it does damage. A bullet to the head hurts and hits, but takes several instances of taking damage to actually kill. Killing requires accumulating damage across multiple hits/instances, not one decisive blow.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1
- Per the harness doc's own texture rule: damage is "banged," not "defeated" — this dump's material is consistent with that framing (see the wedding-lake sequence in arcs/arc2.md, where the target is "banged straight," not killed, by an ordinary hit).
- **Restated for rad (2026-10-07): the health box.** "you stab a rat in the neck, and they take damage. Then they stop beating you. And you have to stab them repeatedly. You stab in the eye, they take damage. You stab in the towards the heart, you push hard, they take damage. And they have a built in health box, and they're just taking damage. You punch them, they eat the punch, and then throw a punch and start throwing punches." So this is not "a world where you can be slim? And still win with daggers."
- **What wins under this rule.** "So the the best at stealing damage and being hard to block becomes the dominant and that's what Keegan is at its soul." (Keegan: characters/.)
  source: raw/2026-10-07-story-world-building-notes.md, msg 1

## Weapon/style variety
- Guns and swords exist and are used conventionally by some fighters.
- Fist-fighters exist; when their punches miss, they still do damage because the hits are effectively AOE ("it'll still do damage because it's, like, AOE fist punches or hits").
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶1

See characters/loading.md for the full breakdown (build-up time, no-spam limitation, close-range double-arm variant, and the counter-learning ability that makes it hard to counter even when predictable).

## RAD powers (arc 2 "metamorphosis")
- Starting in arc 2, many/most RAD develop actual powers — "superpowers, supernatural powers." Framed as logically earned, not arbitrary: they make sense "if you consider how much effort they're putting into manipulating and manifesting those powers" — not just thought/intent, but gestures, movements, and deliberate training.
- Consistent with the harness doc's rule: "MODs are personal and physically logical extensions of effort, not arbitrary powers."
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶2

## RAD dying-to-fight / fighting-to-not-die loop
- RAD have to fight to avoid dying and getting sick — "a healthy amount of fighting" is implied to be necessary/normal for them.
- Some RAD instead choose to be SICK and let themselves take damage until they die, at which point their bodies start attacking anything nearby on their own: "they will be still until a body is in physical LOS. It activates the body to just go and do damage, go and do damage, go and do damage." Outcome in that state is purely a matter of skill.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶2
- See mechanics/sick.md for the SICK/laserstorm mechanic itself, including the "strongest RAD used to just be deadly and reborn quickly, even quick enough to respawn right before a territorial boundary war ends" detail.
  source: raw/2026-07-16-brainstorm-session.md, ¶2
