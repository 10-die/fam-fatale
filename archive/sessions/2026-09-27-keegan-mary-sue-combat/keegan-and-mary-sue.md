# Keegan & Mary Sue: The Field

> Early era. Keegan is still ruthless.
> This is the story of two people who push each other away just by standing close, and what it costs them to stop.

---

## The Core Image

Keegan charges. Mary Sue doesn't run this time. She stands still, stone cold sober, with no one else's radiation in her.

Keegan hits a wall that isn't there.

There's a radius around her. Once he's inside it his body goes **inert**. His momentum dies, his muscles won't answer, his fists stop in the air. He can see her and he can't reach her. The field only drops when **she attacks**. If she wants a fight, she has to open the door herself.

What Keegan doesn't realize is that it goes both ways. When Mary Sue drifts close enough, she feels *his* radiation pushing her back. It's a buzz, a hum, a pressure against her skin. **They are repelling each other.** He thinks she's untouchable. She thinks he's unbearable. Both of them are right, and neither of them knows why.

---

## The Chase Loop

Before the standoff there was the chase, and it's been going on for a long time.

- Mary Sue **bolts**: out of the park, up a fire escape, over a roofline, gone.
- Keegan has to **find her again**. Every time.
- When he does, the fight is **over in seconds**. She lands one clean exchange and disappears.

That's what eats at Keegan. It isn't losing. It's that **he barely gets to fight.** Every encounter is a trailer for a movie she never lets him watch.

Keegan's defining trait is the one that sets him apart from every other character of his archetype: **he doesn't stop.** He doesn't back down, cool off, or walk away, and he does this as a matter of principle, not as a tactic. So when it stops working, he doesn't quit. **He adapts.** That tension (a man who won't stop having to learn to hold still) drives the whole arc.

---

## How Mary Sue Moves

When Keegan does get close and throws something vicious, **her body does weird shit.**

She moves like someone infused ballet, gymnastics, and breakdancing into one nervous system. She folds under a hook, rolls through a handspring, spins out on one hand and comes up behind him. She's a **dynamo**, and nothing about her motion is where you expect it to be.

It isn't radiation. **It's the drugs.** She amplifies herself chemically, on purpose, because she refuses the other option.

### Why she won't take radiation

Taking another rad's radiation doesn't just power you up. **It rewrites you.** You start becoming *them*, or more precisely the version of them that would exist **if they'd lived your life.** Their nature grown through your backstory.

The result is a **hybrid**: something that exists nowhere else, born in that moment, and permanently paired with its source. Willingly or not, the two become **a duo.**

> **One takes damage. The other feels it.**

Mary Sue has seen what that does to people. She'd rather burn herself out on chemistry than become half of somebody else.

---

## The Turn: Keegan Stops

This is where the harsh version of the story starts.

Keegan **stops**. He stops charging and swinging and just stands there.

Mary Sue gets closer than she ever has. Close enough to feel the buzz **without being hit.** She isn't absorbing his radiation or being changed by it. She's just *feeling* it, for the first time, without it arriving as violence.

And her behavior changes, because **Mary Sue is exhausted.**

She is not a rad. She doesn't regenerate mid-fight. She doesn't recover stamina while running, jumping, or flying between buildings. Every vault and spin has been drawn from a tank that doesn't refill. The chase that Keegan found frustrating has been **slowly killing her.**

---

## The Car

This is a key sequence. Play it slow, then brutally fast.

1. Mary Sue throws herself into a car that's already pulling away. It might be someone she trusts, someone she just met, or someone who happened to be there.
2. She's on the floor of the back seat with her cheek on the mat, feeling the road through her skull. **She breathes.** *Holy shit. I'm safe.*
3. A beat of pure quiet: the engine hum, the city sliding past.
4. **Impact.** A crash she hears before she understands it. The vehicle stops.
5. Hands drag her out of the wreck, and **she's fighting again** before she's fully standing.

The one moment she let herself rest got taken from her. That violence doesn't just pass through her. **Something inside her drinks it.**

---

## Loden

### Conception by necessity

At some point **bionics are put inside Mary Sue's body.** It could be a field fix, a survival measure, or something done to her. Whatever the reason, it **triggers a birth.** Something starts growing.

What's growing is **taking the damage.**

Mary Sue isn't just being beaten while this happens. She's fighting, running, and moving the whole time. **She's getting pregnant while kicking ass.** The energy of every hit and every crash, like the car, **feeds Loden's growth.**

### The union

She refuses to let Keegan use her as a **damage sponge**, a place to dump his violence. She fights to survive on her own terms, and out of that she **forms a union with Keegan.** It's a working pairing, not a surrender.

### The birth

**Keegan is there when Loden is born.**

Loden comes out of the womb and **attacks Keegan immediately.** No first breath, no cry, just a fight.

Everything erupts. The radioactive energy at the center spikes so hard that **the entire kingdom starts marching toward it.** The rads, the Keegans, and the humans all converge on one point.

---

## Sick

**Sick is always there.** If not in person, then in attention.

When Sick arrives anywhere, it **downloads.** *Boom.* Heavy. It's an explosion of **energy sap** that pulls out the charge that fires your synapses and moves your muscles. Sick takes that data and **does what it wants with it.**

### Getting caught in a download

The stages go like this:

1. **Euphoria and dread**, mixed together and arriving at the same time.
2. **Hallucinations** follow.
3. Then you're **fried.** You need to sleep.
4. And you start **dream fighting.**

### Dream fighting

You're in a video game. Eyes closed, you move cleanly wherever you need to go. You're smooth and frictionless: a **drive-by**, a try for the one-shot, *bang*, gone.

It's a different zone, **high amp.** In it you **heal while you fight** and gain stamina from fighting instead of losing it. The healing is specifically for **rads**, and it only works when they **share their radiation** with you.

If **Sick catches you** while you're dream fighting, you stop playing. **You become a murder machine.**

---

## Loden and Sick: Open Comms

When Loden and Sick interact, it's **as if they've already been talking.** There are no introductions and no discovery. It's like they've been on an open channel the whole time and one of them finally said something out loud. **That's what makes it scary.**

The idea underneath it: Sick can take any one human, or any thousand. But on the day every human on the planet moves against it at once, *together*, all of them creatures in their prime, all in the same direction, the math stops belonging to Sick. Somewhere in that mass is someone having the best day of their life. Compound the odds across every one of them and **Sick doesn't get to win. It gets to watch the inevitability arrive.** And it can't work out how it's aware of this *while* it's happening.

Loden feels watched by something it can't locate.

**None of that should be said directly.** Here's a draft of how it might sound instead:

---

> *Loden stands in what's left of the square. Everywhere, in every direction, the footsteps are coming. Loden isn't looking at any of them.*
>
> "You heard that too." *(to no one; not a question)*
>
> *A pause, the length of someone else's answer.*
>
> "No, I'm not counting them for you. You counted before I did. You always count first."
>
> *Loden flexes one hand and watches the fingers close, like checking a gauge.*
>
> "Take me off the board. Go on, do it in your head. Count again."
>
> *Beat.*
>
> "Same number, isn't it."
>
> *The footsteps are closer now: boots, bare feet, wheels, something heavier.*
>
> "They don't have to be good. None of them has to be good. They just all have to show up on the same day. And somewhere in there..." *(Loden almost smiles)* "...somebody's having the best day of their life. You can't find which one. Neither can I. That's the whole trick."
>
> *Loden turns suddenly, the way you turn when you're sure someone's behind you. There's nothing there. Just the angle someone would've stood at.*
>
> "Every time I turn around, I just miss you turning around."
>
> *Loden faces forward again.*
>
> "You should come watch this part in person. It's the only part you don't get to change."
>
> *Something in the static around them might be laughter, or it might just be the city holding its breath.*

---

Why it works:

- **"You counted before I did"** establishes the open comms: they've been talking long before this scene.
- **"Take me off the board. Count again. Same number"** is "if I die, how many humans are left?" without ever saying it. The answer is: all of them.
- **"The best day of their life"** is the standard deviation. In a big enough crowd of creatures in their prime, someone will be the outlier, and the outlier is what kills you.
- **"Every time I turn around, I just miss you turning around"** is *"I see it seeing me. I don't know where it is."* It's mutual surveillance with no location.
- **"The only part you don't get to change"** is Sick watching inevitability instead of controlling it.

---

## Back to the Heart of It: Learning Mary Sue

Everything above builds back to the original idea.

### What Keegan has to learn, the hard way

To interact with Mary Sue at all, **Keegan has to stop fucking around.**

- **Let her get close.** When Mary Sue feels safe, she closes the distance immediately. No coaxing needed.
- **Don't take advantage.** If Keegan tries to capitalize on her closeness by striking without earning it, **she hits him first.** The field is honest in both directions.
- **Be in her company without hurting her.** Keegan starts to *interact*. He fights alongside her instead of against her.

### The cruel mechanic

Then the knife twist:

> **The only way to heal Mary Sue is for Keegan to beat her.**

Because now they're bonding. His energy **heals** her, and it also **beats his radiation into her**, which is the exact thing she's refused her whole life. Every recovery makes her a little more hybrid and a little more half of a duo. Every time she's saved, she becomes less only herself.

So the thing that keeps her alive is the thing she ran from, and the man who has to give it to her is the one she spent the whole story outrunning.

### What Keegan actually wants

Keegan still wants a **fight.** A real one, big and intense, the one the chase always denied him.

The difference now is that he's willing to **build up to it.** Patience, proximity, trust, then the fight that means something.
