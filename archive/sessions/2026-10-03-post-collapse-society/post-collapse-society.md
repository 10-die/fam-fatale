# The Recharge — world notes

> Captured from a voice-memo brainstorm. Sections marked **Idea** are the
> original vision. Sections marked **Possible direction** are suggestions to
> react to. Keep them, cut them, or rework them.

---

## The core image

A group of adults come home. They've been fighting at the border, or fighting
**sick**, or **rad**. They come back *unstable*. They don't know what they
want, but they keep trying to do things. One climbs onto a bed and says:

> "I'm uncomfortable. I don't know what I want."

Their thoughts are full of fog.

The **children** are the ones who keep things running. They act like recharge
stations: kids go looking for adults to *recharge* them. They put whimsy back
into the adults and undo the disillusionment, showing them how beautiful life
can be through a child's eyes.

The tone to aim for is **beautifully strange**: children in a crisis society
who are still children. They still have fun, and they handle the stress by
working together.

## The Returned (adults)

**Idea**
- They come back from the border, from sick, or from rad.
- They're restless and want things without knowing what. They're stuck in loops
  of trying and failing to settle.
- Sleep is the most dangerous time. A sleeping adult can **turn sick**.

**Possible direction**
- Give the fog a visible tell: half-finished actions, repeated phrases, objects
  picked up and put down. Readers learn to spot when an adult is sliding.
- Make it a spectrum. *Fogged* means recoverable with recharging. *Drifting*
  means the kids should stay in pairs. *Turning* means get out now.
- The adults may *know* they need the kids and feel ashamed of it. That's a
  source of tenderness and friction.

## The Children

**Idea**
- They're the responsible ones. A lot is automated, but kids handle the parts
  of the world that need hands and judgment, like getting dinner ready together.
- Ages run from very young to teens. Everyone does a part.
- They grow up fast because **they die so often**.
- **Personal streaming network:** kids co-stream to each other and coordinate
  tasks live. Sometimes that means fighting the adults back. Sometimes it means
  escaping.

**Possible direction**
- **Recharging is the thing children are for.** It isn't a cute side effect.
  The society depends on it, and the kids know it.
- Co-streams could work like a game lobby: roles, callouts, a younger kid
  calling "he's mumbling again" while an older one guides the escape route.
  That gives you an easy way to show play and survival in the same frame.
- **What if children *are* respawns?** People come back as children, and
  adulthood is what happens to the ones who survive long enough to be sent
  out and exposed. That would explain why kids are stable, why they grow up
  fast, and why the adults are the broken ones. A kid might be recharging
  someone who used to be *their* kid.

## Sick

**Idea**
- Sick starts and spreads like a wildfire, and it gets snuffed out.
- Humanity throws *all* of its resources at every outbreak.
- When someone turns, **an alarm rings across the entire human country.**

## The agent cascade

**Idea**
- Within seconds, a human-killing agent arrives to land the **first blow**.
  It's hopefully saving lives, and it **probably loses**.
- Then 4 agents. Then 12. It cascades into a flood. Agents deploy one at a
  time, *spammed, spammed, spammed*, until they saturate the area.
- Agents are **already in transit** when the alarm sounds.

**Transit**
- Trains are always zipping across the country.
- Your car **breaks off** from the line and slows down, slows down, slows down…
- …until a **shuttle** delivers you to a terminal at your destination.
  The shuttle is built to absorb the deceleration energy.

**Possible direction**
- The first agent knows they're the opening sacrifice. "First blow" could be a
  title, a rotation, or an honor.
- Kids escaping a turned adult might pass agents sprinting the other way. That
  shot shows the whole country converging on one house.

## Erase and rebuild

**Idea**
- After sick is eliminated, the area is destroyed and rebuilt over a few days.
- A **new blueprint** is drawn up and built in pieces. It's a whole new place
  with **specific alterations**, probably lessons learned from the outbreak.
- Everyone who was saved is brought back.
- Then the **next wave of respawns** arrives.

**Possible direction**
- Neighborhoods are never older than the last outbreak. Kids might remember
  "the old layout" the way we remember a house we moved out of.
- The alterations show how the society learns: a wider hallway here, no
  bedroom doors that lock, sleep pods with sensors.

## The cloning protocol

**Idea**
- Cloning and respawning are **the only thing keeping humanity from going
  extinct**, along with the constant effort to curb sick.
- There **must be a hard lock**. Nobody can make a hundred, or a million, of
  the same person.

**Possible direction: candidate hard locks** (pick one, or combine them)

1. **One living instance.** Each person's pattern can only be instantiated
   while no other instance is alive. Respawn needs a confirmed death, and the
   protocol won't run otherwise. It's simple and legible.
2. **Duplicates breed sick.** The lore reason nobody breaks the lock: two
   living copies of one pattern is how sick *starts*. The world's worst
   disaster is the enforcement mechanism, so nobody needs a law.
3. **Single key.** Each person has one irreplaceable continuity key. The body
   holds it while you're alive, and the vault reclaims it when you die.
   Copying it destroys it.
4. **Scarcity queue.** Bodies take real time, energy, and biomass. There's a
   respawn *queue*, which explains "the next wave," and the queue itself is a
   political question everyone has a stake in.
5. **Every respawn costs something.** Each return loses a little memory or
   ages the pattern, so infinite copies would be infinitely diluted.

Options 1 and 2 together are the strongest: a clear rule, plus a terrifying
reason it's never broken.

## A government of everyone

**Idea**
- **The government is everyone.** Everyone has an equal stake.
- If you have a good idea, people go with you. If you don't, they immediately
  join someone else or start their own.
- Change happens when an individual has an idea that an **overwhelming
  majority** agrees with.
- That's why the response to sick is total. "The government" collapsing all of
  its resources onto an outbreak *is* everyone choosing to.

**Real-world thinkers who've wrestled with this**
- **Athenian direct democracy**: citizens voted directly, and officials were
  often chosen by lot.
- **Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970)**: the "join someone
  else" mechanic is *exit*, and arguing for your idea is *voice*.
- **Paul-Émile de Puydt, "Panarchy" (1860)**: people freely join and leave
  governments the way they switch service providers.
- **Liquid democracy**: you vote directly or delegate your vote to someone, and
  you can take it back instantly. This is very close to the vision above.
- **Murray Bookchin, libertarian municipalism / communalism**: governance by
  face-to-face assemblies of everyone.
- **Peter Kropotkin, *Mutual Aid* (1902)**: cooperation as a survival strategy.
  This fits the kids working together.
- **Jean-Jacques Rousseau, the "general will"**: the classic attempt to define
  what it means for *everyone* to be the sovereign, including its dangers.

**Possible direction: the tension worth writing**
- What happens to the minority when an overwhelming majority moves?
- Do children have an equal stake? If they're the ones holding society
  together, that could be the story's political spine.
- Do fogged adults get a voice?

## Scene seeds

- **Dinner.** Six kids from age five to fifteen make dinner on a co-stream
  while an adult wanders the kitchen asking what they're supposed to want.
- **The recharge.** A small kid climbs onto the bed next to an unsettled adult
  and narrates the ceiling cracks as a map of a dragon kingdom until the
  adult laughs.
- **Night watch.** Kids take shifts watching a sleeping adult's vitals on the
  stream. A twitch, a call, and everyone is up and moving.
- **The alarm.** It rings across the whole country. The kids run out, and the
  first agent's car is already peeling off the train line.
- **The new house.** The rebuilt neighborhood has a wider staircase and a
  window where there wasn't one. A respawned kid walks through it for the
  first time.

## Open questions

- What exactly are **sick** and **rad**? Are they the same thing at different
  stages, or two separate threats?
- What is "the border," and what's on the other side?
- Who or what are the agents: volunteers, a profession, respawns on rotation?
- Do kids keep memories across deaths?
- What's the society called? What do people call the country?
