# Keegan, Sick, and how the sick spreads

Brainstorm, 2026-10-05 (part 2). Follows on from `2026-10-05-rad-fighting-culture.md`.

---

## 1. The big realization: two siblings

> "I've been describing two different characters, Sick and Keegan... because in the beginning, Sick and Keegan were siblings."

- **Keegan** fights at sick-level intensity **without being sick**.
- **Sick**, Keegan's sibling, has been infected so deeply and for so long that they *are* the sick. They don't know what it's like not to be sick. Their old personality has **fused** with it. Not permanently, but un-fusing would take them offline for a long time.
- So the balcony scene in part 1 (spinning kick, kill feed, corpse launched so hard it throws the thrower back into the building) is **Sick**. The restless, probing, never-disengaging hunter is **Keegan**. They're two versions of the same family talent: one sober, one infected.

## 2. Keegan's family and the sick

- **Keegan doesn't get sick**, even while fighting the sick leaders of the other territories, kingdoms, feudal republics, and so on.
- **Keegan's family has to consume sick** to keep up with Keegan.
- **The rest of Degucci territory refuses sick.** They don't want to need the kill to survive, and needing the kill is what sick does to you.
- The **strongest Degucci who *are* sick isolate themselves.** They don't fight each other, because a fight between them sets off a **chain reaction**: a vicious, disgusting, revolting battle. *Revoltingly alluring.* The sound is like a force of nature, belonging to the place where it's happening: rad singing, rapping, humming, whistling, and la-la-ing together while they fight. (This is the dark version of the Brawl.)
- **What this means for the story (still open):** the sick Degucci are **frustrated**. They have to be sick, because sober it's much harder to reach Keegan's level of aggression, where **fear pushes you forward instead of holding you back**. Keegan gets there naturally. That resentment is a good source of conflict, both inside the family and in the territory.

## 3. How Keegan fights

**The image to aim for: Keegan is perceived like a lion.** Picture a real lion in Keegan's place, one brave enough to take on anything. Not because it has no fear, but because it **understands fear and reasons with it**: "If I engage and stand still, a sniper just shoots me. So I don't stand still."

- **No juking, no dodging for show.** Keegan just moves very well, and with intent, both in how they move and in how they *don't*.
- **Start-stop.** They start moving, then freeze when you go for something, then lunge. You can't predict what comes next.
- **The vertical mix-up.** Keegan jumps straight up. Now you have to choose: they might drop and punch, or jet off in whichever direction they punch toward. You run, and that might still happen, so you dodge...
- **"Keegan didn't predict it."** You dodge, and Keegan is already spinning. It *looks* like a read. It wasn't. Keegan simply decided to spin instead of dashing straight, and threw their body so that it **clips** whoever they're chasing.
- **Moon jumps with weight.** Not floaty. Up high and down fast, with a **thud that bounces the ground**. Jump, jump, dash in, dash back, and **watch how they react.**
- **Targeting rule:** Keegan probes until something *reacts*. The ones who react and fight back, instead of stopping to think, are the ones most likely to beat Keegan if the fight dragged on. **So those are the ones Keegan picks.**
- **Slow reveal.** Keegan should look simple at first, and over time show themselves to be *very* thoughtful in how they fight.

### The snowball: the fight is the heal
- Keegan wanders into a scene, runs toward people, runs away, circles, and waits until something reacts. Then Keegan fights the hell out of it.
- Once beaten, that thing **chases Keegan**. Keegan runs to the next fight. Now the first opponent is fighting Keegan *and* fighting alongside Keegan at the same time.
- **Fighting is what heals Keegan.** So the beaten opponents both heal Keegan and add to the damage, pinning the next target down the way they once pinned Keegan.
- Beat by beat: you jump, and the thing Keegan beat earlier grabs your leg and slams its hand into the wall, locking you there. Keegan, who was squatting while all of this happened, is already in the air, already spinning, and kicks you into the wall. You bounce and land on the ground in front of them. *(Maybe no windows in this setting, just walls.)*
- **Contagious engagement:** "Who the fuck is this?" You throw a punch. Keegan punches back. It's fun, and there's a connection. You start becoming more like Keegan, running in *with* Keegan. Keegan dashes in, you slide in beside them, and you pull attention. Keegan gets the very thing that sick gets easily, *total attention* ("I will chase you and keep inting on you"), but Keegan gets it through engagement instead of infection.

### The "What would Keegan do?" test
Goal: when Keegan shows up, people on Reddit and YouTube make content about **what Keegan would do against anything**.

- **Vs. Megatron on a ship overhead:** Keegan paces restlessly. *How do I get up there? Run underneath the ship. If he does nothing, run back and find a plane to crash into it.* Then Keegan actually does it: hops the airport fence, punches through a hangar, gets the plane, crashes it into the ship.
- **Vs. Darth Vader:** Vader sees something getting bigger as it runs straight at him. He uses the Force to stop Keegan a meter or two out. Keegan stops, hits the invisible wall, and runs sideways. Vader lifts Keegan and starts choking. Keegan makes noises, then just *looks* at Vader without breathing, moving their body to test whether they can touch the ground, whether there's any way to break the energy holding them up. *(What happens next is open, and that's fine. What matters is the type of interaction.)*
- **The pattern:** the first time anyone meets Keegan is the first time they fight. If you're out of reach, say on a spaceship looking down, Keegan **runs off, does a bunch of things, and comes back with an army**, made of the people they beat on the way.

## 4. The war: sick vs. Degucci, and the relapse

1. **The push.** The sick territories (the McConnell and others) want to make every Degucci sick, so the Degucci stop blocking them every time they try to run into the human territories.
2. **The Degucci block.** Each time, the Degucci run across the border and beat them. They're a **tighter collective** than the McConnell and the other territories.
3. **Beating the sick out.** The Degucci beat the sick out of their enemies, and it cascades. Knocked-out sick wake up *not* sick, and turn to fight the sick alongside the Degucci. (Same snowball as Keegan's style, but across a whole army.)
4. **The aftermath.** The war ends and everyone goes home. The formerly sick territories are now sober. They need rest and recovery so they can become rad like the Degucci.
5. **The relapse.** But there's still **one sick** left there, and everyone just fights it. Someone thinks, *"I want to be strong. Give me your sick, share it with me."* Then the cascade runs the other way: *"I'm not fighting you lot, you'll kill me. I'll be sick too."* And they all become sick again.

This is a strong tragic loop: liberation doesn't last unless the culture changes.

## 5. What sick *is*

- Sick makes everyone use whatever they have in the **most lethal, dangerous, high-impact way possible**.
- **The gaming analogy:** think of the gap between a bronze player and the top 10 ranked lobbies on any character. Sick is that gap. Any creature, played as if by the most cracked-out player you know from any field, all mixed together. *Same creature, now oriented toward fighting well*, whether or not it ever fought before.
