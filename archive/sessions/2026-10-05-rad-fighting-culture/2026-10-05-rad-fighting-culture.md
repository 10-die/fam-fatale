# Rad fighting culture: movement, the Brawl, and the sick

Brainstorm, 2026-10-05. Kept close to how it was first said. Open questions are at the bottom.

## 1. Scene: the balcony (told from the human's point of view)

- Two humans are holed up in an apartment room, trying to fight and survive together.
- **Sick** turns away from our human and throws a spinning jump kick. The kick sends our human over the balcony.
- The last thing they see from inside: Sick leaping at the *other* human, who has only just got back up.
- The camera falls with our human. They hit the ground, start to get up, and look up.
- **The kill feed** says Sick killed the other human. We don't see the death. We only read it.
- Then the corpse comes out of the building, thrown so hard that Sick flies back *into* the building from the recoil. Sick moves through the structure, kicking walls and doing damage along the way.

The horror of the scene: the death that matters most happens off-screen and shows up as text.

## 2. Rad movement: friction as a variable

Rad with radioactive blood can change how physics applies to them, at the level of the math, the *variables*. So they can:

- **Skip, then glide.** When their feet touch the ground, friction can drop to near zero. They slide, glide, and skate across surfaces like they're tap dancing.
- **Snap-turn.** Friction can spike suddenly, so they can change direction on a dime **and keep their momentum**.
- **Spend momentum only on a strike.** Momentum is kept until they hit something, and the hit carries all of it.

### The drop strike
- The rad drops from somewhere high and aims to land right next to something alive.
- They bend their legs on landing and punch down into the ground. The force goes into the point where the fist hits.
- Anything close enough takes the damage of a fall from that height, or a bit less. In every case it's in the lethal range.
- A small version works from an ordinary jump: jump near someone, then smash the ground.
- **The weakness:** they're vulnerable on the way down. A hit during the descent cancels the strike and gives whoever landed it the advantage.

### Writing principle
Fights should last several moments, so **everyone in the fight gets their chance to try something**. The drop strike's weakness, along with commitment and counters, is what makes room for that.

## 3. The Brawl: rad culture as music

Where it came from: a kindergarten concert four houses down. The whole neighborhood agreed to keep the music going because people were enjoying something together.
> Culture is a collective's personhood. It's what lets a whole neighborhood agree to keep the music pumping, because they're having a blast together.

The rad version:

- **Rad make music with their bodies while they fight.** Every hit is a beat, and every beat comes from a different song.
- Together they make a racket that **slowly crawls from one style to another**. A few bars of one vibe, then something completely different, always shifting.
- From a distance you hear a fight over here and another over there, so you know a big group of rad is brawling. **If you join, you become part of the music.**
- They're **brawling with love**. They aren't sick, so they aren't trying to eliminate anyone. You beat someone up, take a beating, and move on to the next fight.
- **They gain stamina as they fight.** The intensity keeps rising until it hits a crescendo. The music gets loud, and that pulls in smaller clusters nearby, and the energy builds even more.
- Kids stop singing and go home. **The Brawl doesn't stop.** It keeps building and drawing people in.
- Clashing styles start fighting each other (say, a Southern twang group against something else). Then they **change together** and begin to blend on purpose.
- It moves from damage to understanding. First they just try to hurt each other. Then they start to read each other and **build moves together**. One moment you're fighting a stranger. The next you're fighting beside them against a few others.
- The bodies: sweating, heaving, **breathing radioactive blood out as vapor** into the air. They're in their prime.
- **Agency and drift.** You can still stop, stand still, and admire something before springing back in. You control what you do, but not the *change* itself: "I'm gonna do this — whoa, I'm dropping down a level — oh, I'm fighting these people now — good kick — dodged it."
- **Mary Sue** lives as far from the Brawl as she can, because being near it means you have to interact with everything.

## 4. The sick, and the McConnell

- When the **sick** take over, as with **the McConnell**, fighting turns into a *business*. Fights become formal 1v1 challenges.
- They don't kill each other because they're crazy. They kill because **they know death isn't final**.
- **Death in this world:** when you die, your awareness keeps going. You're stuck watching whoever beat you. Whatever you see makes you furious, so **you come back with a vengeance. And salt.**

## 5. Deguccis territory

(More detail lives in other, non-Code Claude sessions. It needs to be brought in here.)

- **Leaders of anarchy** who form **collectives** for whatever needs doing right now.
- **Anarchy means being extra.** If you want things to change for the better, you have to be extra: start fights and build collectives.
- A collective is people who understand how deep your damage goes and *choose* to follow you. They fight beside you and **give you their damage** in exchange for your direction. You move, and they follow.
- **Disengaging is normal.** Mid-fight, everyone might just leave. ("They've been kicking your ass for a while. 'Ah, fuck off.' 'Alright, let's go.'")

Sick territories, kingdoms, and other powers:
- the McConnell
- kingdoms
- a technopolis

Rad economic life:

- Then they **lock in and build**: buildings, structures, and contraptions that move them around those structures and *shoot them* across their own architecture. (This ties straight into the friction and momentum movement.)
