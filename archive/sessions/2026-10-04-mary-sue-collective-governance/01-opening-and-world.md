# Opening + World Notes (brain dump, organized)

## What we're actually trying to do

Write the **opening of the story**. It's a journey, but told through the *world*, not one person:

- Multiple POVs, passing the camera from person to person.
- Some of them we see again next episode. Some not until season two. Some never again.
- **We start on Mary Sue.** Her thread: an addiction to being alone and fighting alone.

## Mary Sue — the opening thread

- She refuses to belong to any collective. No government, no co-ownership.
- She needs rad blood (radioactive "drugs"). There are two ways to get it:
  1. **Buy it on the free market**: the economy of the anarchic collective. No head, but there is order, and the residents enforce it themselves.
  2. **Make it herself.** That's what she's doing: experimenting with homemade rad blood so she depends on nothing and no one.
- Her addiction is to independence itself. The drug is just how it shows.

## The world: how Earth changed

### Government = everyone
- Every human (or rad) who belongs to a collective is a part owner of it.
- "We no longer have egos of one. We have egos of collectives."
- Contrast: Westeros/King's Landing, life under one dominator surrounded by other dominators. This world is built against that.

### The King (origin of the new order)
- It started with one person who livestreamed their whole life and struggle.
- They streamed themselves studying for the bar, with chat helping: "how do we do this?"
- The goal was to understand how law can make everyone help everyone. *Whatever you do is for everyone. What everyone does is for everyone.*
- Chat spams things like "I rent, can I just own the place I live in?" The King runs the math live, and it passes by consensus.
- That's why the King is absolute: the King has absolute power *because* they represent everyone. People adopted it because it actually changed their lives.
- It wiped out the elite class. Almost everyone now sits between lower and upper middle class.
  - Middle class = you get to interact with the economy at regular intervals.
  - Lower class = people resting and recovering, living in the center of human territory.
- ⚠️ Tension to use: the King also used fear of the **sick** to turn humans against rads (see below).

### Housing, jobs, education
- One house per person, to live in and not to sell. Rent doesn't exist anymore.
- A job anchors you to your house. Lose the job and you keep the house **as long as you keep applying**.
- School is a safe place to find the thing that makes you go "fuck yeah, I'm a cool human." Then you get paid for it.
  - Example: a kid who liked EGD drawings designs a shed and it's pitched to backyard/lawn companies. If one buys it, that pays for the education.
  - Education is a loan, and you can borrow against it to get a house.
- Losing a job is "I've got a few interviews lined up," not "I'm sleeping in my car."

### Economy
- Big business is gone. Big business is now the government, and everyone is the government.
- Small business only: mom-and-pop, uncles, a bunch of cousins. One business per group of people, run because they're good at it and love it, and they charge for it.
- Small business doesn't make a few people rich. It makes a lot of people stable.

## The sick (the threat)

- Clones are raised in the **center of human territory**. They grow to adults in days, but for those few days they're kids.
- **Sick adults** can't hide it. They go into a frenzy.
- **Sick children lie.** They become spies whose intentions can't be detected.
- How it spreads: a moment alone together, then a death. Now there are two of them, both pretending. There's no corpse because the corpse is walking around.
- The virus doesn't die when the host dies. It reads the brain's synapses and memories, *experiences* the host's history, and becomes the character so completely that you can't tell the difference.
- In a living host it fights the conscious mind, mutes the person's thoughts and rewrites them.
- It gives itself away when someone says "you're acting weird." Then the game's over and it has to kill.
- **Core dread:** you can only deal with it *after* it happens. Before that it's speculation, and that speculation is what makes humans hostile to rads.

## Themes floating around

- Trauma passed down: hurt people raising people. "A scar where you can see the creases in your cells aren't there."
- Disconnection: people harass strangers because they only feel tied to a few people.
- The old world as a contrast: renting, running on a string, cops knocking on the car window.
