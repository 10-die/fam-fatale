# Narrative Structure

## No protagonist, no antagonist

There are **no main characters.** We follow **groups**, and the camera hands off between them. Be consistent about this.

## The relay

1. Follow **one person in a group.**
2. They interact with someone, and **the perspective switches.** Now we're hanging out with *that* person.
3. Move **through the group** until we understand their bond and see them work together.
4. The group runs into **another group.** We see faces and a few behaviors.
5. That group runs into **another group.** Something happens.
6. Eventually we **return to the first character we followed.** Now they're in conflict with someone close to a member of the second group.
7. **One beats the other.** Who wins doesn't really matter.

## Staying with the loser

- Say the second group's guy loses. The character we've been following leaves, but **the camera stays with the knocked-out guy who tried.** We saw them try. Maybe they made an error. They got cooked.
- **Their group comes to them and comforts them**, holds them, and gathers around.
- Then they start **shit-talking the other group.** Some of it is rumor, some of it is true, and the audience knows which.
- *"Oh shit, we're shit-talking the group we were just following. We kind of like those guys."* Whether you like them is subjective, and that's the beautiful part.

## Making them likable

Write them **human**: the joke here, the tease there, the courage to break social tension, being approachable even when they're in a hurry.

## Pacing device: Keegan dread

Keegan is a **pending event.** Scenes fill up with yapping and insults while everyone asks *is Keegan coming?* When Keegan arrives, the talking stops. **It's fists.**
